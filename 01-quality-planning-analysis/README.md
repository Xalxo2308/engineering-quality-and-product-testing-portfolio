# Quality Planning and Analysis

## Project Overview

This case study is based on a Quality Planning and Analysis group assignment completed as part of postgraduate engineering management studies at the University of Technology Sydney (UTS).

The case study examines how quality planning methods can be used to understand customer expectations, prioritise requirements and improve the quality of a service offering.

The original assignment analysed a hypothetical restaurant, Gastronome Haven, using quality management and customer satisfaction methodologies.

## Objectives

- Identify and analyse stakeholder requirements.
- Understand customer expectations and satisfaction.
- Categorise customer needs according to their importance.
- Translate customer requirements into quality and design requirements.
- Apply structured quality analysis methods to support improvement.

## Quality Analysis Methods

### 1. Stakeholder Analysis

Stakeholder analysis was used to identify the expectations and needs of different groups associated with the organisation, including customers, employees, suppliers, investors and other stakeholders.

### 2. Customer Needs Analysis

Customer needs were examined through structured feedback methods, including surveys and feedback forms, and social media listening.

### 3. Kano Model

The Kano Model was used to categorise customer expectations into different levels of satisfaction, helping distinguish essential requirements from performance-related needs and attractive features.

### 4. Quality Function Deployment (QFD)

QFD was used to establish relationships between customer expectations and design or operational requirements. This approach supports the prioritisation of requirements that have the greatest impact on customer satisfaction.

## Key Quality Principles Demonstrated

- Customer-focused quality planning.
- Requirement identification and prioritisation.
- Structured quality analysis.
- Translating customer expectations into measurable requirements.
- Continuous improvement.
- Systematic decision-making.

## Relevance to Product Testing

The methods explored in this project are relevant to product quality and testing because effective testing begins with understanding what a product is expected to do.

Customer expectations and product requirements can be translated into testable criteria. These criteria can subsequently be used to assess functionality, performance, usability, reliability and customer satisfaction.
